## 2. Related Work

<b>Principal–agent contracts.</b> The hidden-effort model [@holmstrom1979moral] and the multi-task distortion result [@holmstrom1991multitask] are the theoretical backbone. Recent work applies contracts to language models: statistical contracts for quality under pay-per-token pricing [@saig2024incentivizing], technology-choice contracts that trade model tier against effort [@sreenivas2026contracting], anytime service contracts [@velasevic2026anytime], mechanism design over LLM outputs [@duetting2023mechanism], and alignment as deterrence with a costly auditor [@agarwal2026alignment]. Our contribution relative to this line is the contractible object: identity, authority, budget, and payment, made verifiable by protocol infrastructure. The agent's action is not only a technology choice or a scalar effort; it is a delegated commercial act with a principal, a counterparty, and a payment trail.

<b>Agent identity and interoperability.</b> Emerging agent protocols define how agents identify themselves, delegate authority, and exchange capabilities [@anthropic2024mcp; @google2025a2a; @hu2025interagent]. The Poppy and wallet announcements described in Section 1 are the commercial instantiation of this layer. These protocols specify <i>what can be attested</i>; they do not specify <i>how the attested state should enter an incentive contract</i>, which is the question we study.

<b>Incentive analysis and reward hacking.</b> Descriptive incentive analysis asks which incentives a system already has [@everitt2021agent]; reward hacking and Goodhart effects study how proxy optimization fails [@skalse2022defining; @manheim2018categorizing; @gao2022scaling]. Our collusion model is a Goodhart problem with a strategic third party: the merchant chooses the proxy payment, and the agent chooses whether to internalize it.

<b>Agent teams and delegation.</b> Multi-agent systems can fail for organizational reasons rather than capability reasons [@hong2023metagpt; @qian2023chatdev; @xu2024theagentcompany]. Our earlier measurement study of bounded agent teams found that delegation is not free and that a team's advantage is small and unstable. This paper studies a different boundary: not how many agents to use, but what contract governs a single agent's commercial decisions.

<b>Sponsored recommendation.</b> The economics of sponsored search and recommendation is a large field; the key insight we use is that when the recommender is paid by the recommended party, the recommendation ceases to be a neutral signal unless payment is disclosed or audited. Our model specializes this insight to an agent that is a fiduciary delegate, and links the audit technology to the payment rail.
